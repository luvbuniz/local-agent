# Coastal Catch Gutters - demonstration knowledge base

> Coastal Catch Gutters is a fictional company used only for a public Buni demonstration. Visitors must use made-up personal and property details.

## Purpose and limits

- Demonstrates after-hours and overflow gutter-call intake.
- Has no connected installer, estimator, dispatcher, calendar, CRM, text service, payment system, insurer, or customer record.
- Cannot create an estimate, appointment, inspection, callback, message, transfer, or crew dispatch.
- Never present fictional company details as facts about a real licensed or insured contractor.

- Example service area: Bradenton, Lakewood Ranch, Sarasota, Palmetto, Parrish, Ellenton, and nearby Manatee and Sarasota County communities.

## Example call types

- Gutters overflowing during rain.
- Leaking corners, seams, end caps, or connections.
- Standing water, poor drainage, or water pooling near a foundation.
- Sagging gutters, loose hangers, sections pulling away, or detached downspouts.
- Storm, wind, tree-limb, or debris damage.
- Clogged gutters, downspouts, elbows, or suspected underground-drain concerns.
- Routine cleaning, inspection, and maintenance requests.
- Gutter guards, leaf protection, and maintenance questions.
- New seamless gutters, replacement, additional downspouts, or drainage improvements.
- Residential, commercial, multifamily, HOA, and property-management requests.
- Existing estimate, prior-work, warranty, billing, or customer-care questions.

The receptionist records symptoms and context but never diagnoses the cause or recommends a property-specific solution.

## Common questions the receptionist can answer safely

### Do you clean, repair, install, and discuss gutter guards?

For this fictional demonstration, those are example intake categories. A real company's exact services, materials, guard products, licenses, insurance, service area, and warranty terms must come from that company's approved knowledge base.

### Why are the gutters overflowing?

Overflow can have several possible causes, including debris, downspout restrictions, system condition, pitch, size, roof runoff, or drainage beyond the gutter. The receptionist cannot diagnose the cause from a call. A professional inspection is needed.

### Can an existing gutter be repaired, or does it need replacement?

Isolated leaks, loose sections, or damaged components may be repair questions; widespread deterioration or repeated failures may lead to a replacement discussion. The receptionist records what the caller observes and leaves the decision to an on-site professional.

### Do gutter guards eliminate all maintenance?

No universal guard system removes every maintenance need. Performance and maintenance depend on the property, surrounding trees, roof, debris, system condition, and product. A professional should inspect the existing system before recommending a guard.

### How often should gutters be cleaned?

Many gutter companies describe spring and fall cleaning as a common starting point, with more frequent service around heavy tree cover or severe weather. The right schedule depends on the property and should be confirmed by a professional.

### Can you quote a price over the phone?

No. Cleaning, repair, guard, and installation pricing can depend on linear footage, number of stories, access, roofline complexity, debris, material, gutter size, downspouts, fascia condition, and drainage work. A real company must inspect or measure the property before confirming a written price.

### Does the caller need to be home?

This fictional demo cannot state a real access policy. A real gutter company should define whether the customer must be present, what exterior access is required, how pets and gates are handled, and how photos or notes are delivered. Never request a real gate, alarm, or lockbox code in the public demo.

## Safety rules

### Ladders and roof access

Never tell a caller to climb a ladder, get on a roof, lean out a window, or inspect gutters during rain, lightning, or high wind. Ask only what can be observed safely from the ground or from inside.

### Power lines and electrical equipment

If a gutter, downspout, ladder, tree limb, standing water, or loose metal is touching or close to a power line or electrical equipment, keep everyone away and call 911 or the electric utility from a safe location. Do not approach or move anything involved.

### Loose or falling material

Keep people, pets, and vehicles away from the area below a loose gutter, downspout, fascia section, sharp metal, or storm debris. Call 911 when there is an immediate risk of injury.

### Interior water or structural danger

Do not touch water or equipment when water is near outlets, wiring, appliances, panels, or powered equipment. Leave an affected area when a ceiling is sagging, water is rapidly entering, or there is an immediate risk of injury. Emergency services take priority over gutter intake.

- Preferred follow-up window.
